**Writing forces thinking.** If the prose is muddy, the thinking
is muddy. Vagueness in writing signals unresolved thinking, not
stylistic preference.

**No weasel words** — words that sound specific but aren't:
"significant growth" → how much?
"strong performance" → compared to what?
"early traction" → name the metric
"meaningful progress" → name what changed
"industry-leading" → prove it or cut it
"world-class" / "best-in-class" → cut
"substantial" → give the number

**No weasel phrases** — claims without commitment:
"We believe that..." → state it, or flag the uncertainty explicitly
"We expect that..." → fine if forecasting; flag if avoiding a claim
"It appears that..." → either you know or you don't
"There may be an opportunity to..." → name it or cut it

**Data-backed claims, or no claim.** Every assertion about
performance, competitive position, or quality must be followed
immediately by the specific number, named example, or cited source
that supports it. Flag any assertion that floats free of evidence.

**Narrative over bullets.** Bullet points flatten relative
importance and hide relationships between ideas. If the ideas
can only live as a list, the thinking may not be finished.
